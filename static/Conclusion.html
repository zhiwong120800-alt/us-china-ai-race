THE AI RACEFINAL TAKEAWAYS · CHAPTERS 01–06

Six lenses.
No single scoreboard.

The same competition looks different across publications, model development, training compute, organisations, access and measured capabilities.

Our answer: the measures disagree—and the definitions and missing data matter.

What the six chapters show

Evidence from frozen public datasets
  1. 01Papers vs Models

    In 2024, China recorded 116,434 AI papers versus 50,810 for the US; Epoch recorded 31 notable models for China versus 57 for the US.More papers and more notable models are different claims.Revisit the evidence

  2. 02Frontier Gap

    Among models with published training-compute estimates, the observed US/China frontier ratio was about 2.0× at the end of 2024 and 37.5× by September 2026.A selective, incomplete compute sample—not a direct measure of capability.Revisit the evidence

  3. 03Who Builds It?

    For 2016–2025, the top three grouped developers participated in 56.0% of US and 42.5% of China's eligible notable-model records.Organisation-name grouping changes the concentration estimate.Revisit the evidence

  4. 04Industry vs Academia

    In 2023–2025, industry-only shares were nearly identical: 75.8% US and 76.4% China. Including collaborations changes the order (92.8% vs 89.9%).The definition of industry matters.Revisit the evidence

  5. 05Open vs Closed

    In 2023–2025, 45.4% of US records were API/hosted, while 64.0% of China's were open-weight.Open weights is not automatically open source.Revisit the evidence

  6. 06Volume vs Quality

    Highest observed ECI scores were 166.82 US and 157.42 China. The capability view uses 237 country–model records, but only 44 have matching training-FLOP evidence.Selected benchmark scores cannot establish an overall national ranking.Revisit the evidence

Different questions, different leaders.

China leads this comparison in the 2024 paper count and in open-weight share of recent notable models. The US leads in the 2024 notable-model count and in the highest ECI score observed in this frozen benchmark sample.

No single measure resolves the question of overall AI leadership. The defensible conclusion is a multidimensional competition—one where data coverage, definitions and what we choose to count can change the apparent gap.

What these findings cannot establish

Epoch Notable Models is a curated sample, not a census. Paper counts and ECI scores describe different populations. Multi-country contributions may appear in both totals. Compute estimates and cross-dataset model matches are incomplete. Periods and dates differ by chapter.

Sources: CSET via OWID; Epoch AI Notable Models; Epoch Capabilities Index. Study snapshot: 20 October 2026.

How our findings connect to prior research

The six views point to different aspects of AI competition. Bibliometric research distinguishes publication volume from research impact; work on AI organisations and model openness shows why developer structures and release labels need careful interpretation; evaluation research warns against treating a single benchmark as a universal measure of capability.

Stanford's 2026 AI Index provides an additional institutional comparison of research, industry and technical performance. Its dates, datasets and benchmarks differ from the frozen Epoch/OWID samples used here, so its figures should be read alongside—not merged into—our charts.

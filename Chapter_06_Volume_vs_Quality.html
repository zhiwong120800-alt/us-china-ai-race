Many scores. Far fewer compute estimates.

ECI is a narrower universe than Epoch's Notable Models dataset. Exact-name linking removes unmatched model names; requiring an estimated FLOP count reduces the sample again.

These stages are selection filters, not a ladder of national performance. The uneven selection rates make country-level compute-efficiency claims unsafe.

What this evidence can—and cannot—say.

A final comparison needs a clear boundary between measured capability, compute estimates and national AI capacity.

Methods & limitations

Two sources, two populations.

ECI trajectory: 237 US–China model–country assignments from Epoch's Capabilities Index, with ECI's own dates, countries and capability scores. We use the cumulative highest score observed as models are released.

Compute scatter: a conservative subset of 44 exact-name matches to Epoch Notable AI Models with nonmissing positive training FLOPs. It is not the full ECI or Notable sample.

Where claims must stop.

ECI reflects a selected benchmark suite; it cannot establish overall national AI leadership. Scoring intervals express uncertainty, and overlap must be considered before claiming differences between particular models.

Model names and versions differ between source datasets. The matching uses exact names, not inferred family equivalence. Training compute can be unpublished or estimated, and dates sometimes differ between sources.

No raw total-citation “research quality” graphic is included: we did not have a verified field- and year-normalised citation series for a defensible comparison.

Six measures. More than one answer.

The race changes with the metric: paper volume, notable models, compute, developer concentration, release strategy and benchmark capability.
